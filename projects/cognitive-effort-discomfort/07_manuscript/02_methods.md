# Methods

This scoping review was conducted following the framework of Arksey and O'Malley (2005), as refined by Levac et al. (2010) and aligned with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for scoping reviews. Reporting follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR; Tricco et al., 2018).

## Protocol

The review protocol was developed a priori and is available in the project repository. The protocol specifies the Population–Concept–Context (PCC) framework, eligibility criteria, search strategy, and charting variables.

## Eligibility Criteria

**Population:** Healthy adults (≥18 years) with no current headache disorder diagnosis, chronic pain condition, or neurological/psychiatric disorder that would confound cognitive effort measurement. Studies with mixed clinical and healthy samples were included if results for healthy participants were reported separately or if the theoretical framework was generalizable.

**Concept:** Studies addressing the intersection of sustained cognitive effort and somatic discomfort, operationalized across five domains: (1) cognitive effort cost mechanisms, (2) neural metabolic consequences of sustained cognition, (3) effort as aversive signal, (4) predictive processing/allostatic accounts of effort, and (5) somatic referral of cognitive fatigue.

**Context:** Sustained cognitive tasks of ≥30 minutes duration (or equivalent sustained-demand paradigms) with a comparison point (rest, low-demand control, or pre-task baseline). Any setting (laboratory, online, simulated workplace). Any study design including empirical research, theoretical papers proposing novel frameworks, and systematic reviews synthesizing directly relevant evidence.

**Exclusions:** Studies exclusively examining clinical headache/pain populations without healthy controls (code P1), pharmacological-only interventions without cognitive task (I2), tasks under 30 minutes without sustained demand manipulation (I1), case reports or editorials (S1), and non-English publications (L1).

## Information Sources and Search Strategy

Three electronic databases were searched on March 22, 2026: PubMed (via systematic web-verified search), Semantic Scholar (citation tracing), and CrossRef (DOI validation and metadata enrichment). The search spanned publications from 2010 to 2026, with exceptions for foundational pre-2010 theoretical work.

Five query sets were constructed to cover each literature layer:
- Query 1 (L1/L3/L5): cognitive effort AND somatic discomfort AND healthy adults
- Query 2 (L2): cognitive fatigue AND neural metabolic markers AND prefrontal cortex
- Query 3 (L4): interoception AND predictive processing AND cognitive effort
- Query 4 (L1/L3): effort discounting AND expected value of control AND cognitive cost
- Query 5 (L5): tension-type headache AND cognitive load AND pain threshold

Exact query strings are provided in the supplementary materials. Additionally, forward and backward citation tracing was performed on eight key references identified a priori from the literature map (Kurzban et al., 2013; Inzlicht et al., 2018; Shenhav et al., 2013, 2017; Wiehler et al., 2022; Christie & Schrater, 2015; Seth & Friston, 2016; Yee et al., 2022).

## Selection of Sources of Evidence

All records were screened independently by two AI-assisted reviewers using pre-specified eligibility criteria. A liberal screening approach was adopted per scoping review methodology: records classified as "maybe" by either reviewer were included for full-text assessment. Inter-rater agreement was calculated using Cohen's kappa.

## Data Charting

Data were extracted into a standardized charting table with 18 variables organized across primary domains (somatic discomfort measures, effort cost measures, neural/metabolic markers, somatic localization, theoretical framework) and secondary domains (task characteristics, sample characteristics, study design, key findings). Each study was classified into one or more of the five literature layers (L1–L5). All charting data were derived from published abstracts, PubMed metadata, and open-access full texts.

## Synthesis of Results

Results were synthesized narratively, organized by the five literature layers. Frequency counts and cross-tabulations of study characteristics were computed. A gap analysis was performed to identify domains where research is absent or insufficient. No formal quality or risk-of-bias assessment was conducted, consistent with JBI guidance for scoping reviews.
