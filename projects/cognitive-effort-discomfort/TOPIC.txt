# Scoping Review Research Topic

## Title
Somatic Discomfort During Sustained Cognitive Effort in Healthy Adults: A Scoping Review

---

## Research Question
What is the nature and extent of existing research on somatic discomfort (including headache-like sensations, fatigue, and bodily discomfort) experienced during or after sustained cognitive effort in healthy adults, and what theoretical frameworks have been used to explain this phenomenon?

---

## PCC Framework (Population-Concept-Context — standard for scoping reviews)

### Population
**Inclusion:**
- Healthy adults (≥18 years)
- No current headache disorder diagnosis (no migraine, no chronic tension-type headache)
- No chronic pain conditions
- No neurological or psychiatric disorders that would confound cognitive effort measures

**Exclusion:**
- Studies exclusively using clinical headache/pain populations (TTH, migraine patients)
- Pediatric populations (<18 years)
- Studies focused solely on pharmacological interventions without cognitive task component

---

### Concept
The intersection of sustained cognitive effort and somatic discomfort, spanning five literature domains:
1. **Cognitive effort cost**: Opportunity cost models, effort paradox, Expected Value of Control (EVC)
2. **Neural metabolic cost**: Glutamate accumulation, glycogen depletion, prefrontal cortex metabolic changes
3. **Effort as aversive signal**: dACC-mediated effort monitoring, effort discounting, aversive motivation
4. **Predictive processing / allostasis**: Free energy principle, active interoceptive inference, interoceptive prediction error
5. **Somatic referral of cognitive fatigue**: Pericranial mechanisms, stress-hyperalgesia, central sensitization in non-clinical populations

---

### Context
- **Task requirement**: Sustained cognitive task ≥30 minutes (N-back, Stroop, sustained attention, working memory, simulated workday)
- **Comparator**: Rest, low-demand control condition, or pre-task baseline
- **Settings**: Laboratory, online/remote, simulated workplace environments
- **Study designs**: Any (RCTs, within-subjects, between-subjects, observational, neuroimaging, computational modeling, theoretical/review papers contributing novel frameworks)

---

## Outcomes of Interest (Charting Variables)

### Primary charting domains:
1. **Somatic discomfort measures**: VAS pain/discomfort, headache ratings, fatigue scales, bodily discomfort inventories
2. **Effort cost measures**: Effort discounting tasks, subjective effort ratings, willingness to exert effort
3. **Neural/metabolic markers**: fMRI activation patterns, MRS glutamate levels, EEG changes, pupillometry
4. **Somatic localization**: Whether discomfort was localized (head, neck, eyes) vs. diffuse/general
5. **Theoretical framework**: Which of the 5 literature layers was invoked to explain findings

### Secondary charting domains:
6. Task type and duration
7. Sample characteristics (N, age, screening criteria)
8. Study design and methodology
9. Key findings (1-2 sentence summary per study)

---

## Methodology
- **Scoping review** following Arksey & O'Malley (2005) framework, refined by Levac et al. (2010) and JBI methodology
- **Reporting**: PRISMA-ScR (PRISMA Extension for Scoping Reviews; Tricco et al., 2018)
- **No formal quality/risk-of-bias assessment** (per JBI scoping review guidance — quality appraisal is optional)
- **Narrative synthesis** organized by the 5 literature layers

## Rationale for Scoping Review (not Meta-Analysis)
- Outcomes are not consistently operationalized across studies (VAS fatigue, effort discounting, PPT, headache diary, self-reported difficulty)
- No common manipulation ('sustained cognitive effort' ranges from 15-min Stroop to 8-hour simulated workday)
- The healthy non-clinical population is almost entirely absent from existing headache literature
- Zero studies have jointly measured interoceptive discomfort localization + effort economics — there is nothing to pool
- A scoping review maps the heterogeneous cross-disciplinary terrain and makes the research gap visible
